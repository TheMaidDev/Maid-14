# Campfire cooking

Only the plain bonfire (`Bonfire`) is a fuelled fire, not a permanent heat source: it starts unlit and empty, has to be fed and lit by hand, and only while burning heats items lying on it, so existing temperature-based recipes cook food over it. The other bonfires (`LegionnaireBonfire`, `RMCCampfire`) share the abstract `BonfireBase` with it but keep the old behaviour: always hot, always burning, nothing cooks on them. Prototypes cannot drop inherited components, which is why the shared parts moved to the base and only `Bonfire` adds the system. Code lives in the `_Maid` folders of Shared and Server (`Campfire`).

## Pieces
- `CampfireComponent` holds remaining burn time, a cap on it, the materials that count as fuel with burn seconds per material unit, heat power per item, a temperature ceiling for heated items, the pickup radius, the item limit, the tick interval, the air heating powers (one per fuel stage), the air temperature ceiling, the oxygen threshold and grace time, and the putting-out rules (reagents, amount, stamping times, spray counts). The lit flag and a coarse remaining-time level (empty plus five fifths of the fuel cap) are networked; exact fuel stays server-side.
- The prototype also carries `PlaceableSurface` (items placed on it land in the centre) and a `GenericVisualizer` that shows the fire layer only while lit. The old `AlwaysHot` is gone from the bonfire; the system answers the "is hot" query itself while lit, so a burning fire still lights cigarettes and torches. Light and ambient fire sound start disabled and follow the lit state.

## Flow
- Using an item on the fire: if unlit and the item is hot (lighter, welder, lit match, anything answering the hot query), the fire lights provided it has fuel (otherwise a "nothing to burn" popup). Otherwise, if the item's physical composition contains a fuel material (wood, cloth, paper), burn time is added and one unit is consumed (one piece of a stack, or the whole item); a full fire refuses. Anything else falls through, so the item is placed on the fire.
- Fuel value is sum over composition of material amount times the configured seconds per unit; a wooden plank is a minute. This reuses material compositions, so any wooden or cloth object works without per-prototype tagging. Items with no material composition at all burn for a fixed 20 seconds if they carry the `Paper` tag (papers, books, mail), so tagged paper counts the same as a sheet of paper material. A blacklist (set on the bonfire: anything with the `Shovel`, `DevilContract` or `RevivalContract` component or the `Trash` tag, which also removes trash made of fuel materials and Trash-tagged paper) and living creatures are never fuel.
- Once per interval a lit fire burns fuel and heats loose items (item component plus temperature component) within the radius, up to the item limit, by adding heat energy until they reach the ceiling. Cooking itself is not new: construction graph steps with a minimum temperature fire on temperature change, using internal temperature when present. Running out of fuel puts the fire out. 
- Examine shows the lit state and the remaining burn time as a range (13-15, 10-12, 7-10, 4-7, 1-3 minutes), one range per fifth of the fuel cap. The texts assume the default 15-minute cap.
- The same tick also warms the air of the fire's tile. Power follows the fuel stage (the fuel range is split into three equal parts: 300, 400, 600 W, fullest stage strongest); atmos spreads the heat to neighbouring tiles. Heating stops at the ceiling (about 40 °C) and each step is limited to the headroom below it, because thin air has almost no heat capacity and would otherwise jump by thousands of kelvin. Immutable mixtures (space, map atmosphere) are skipped. Heat goes in on the scaled atmos heat capacity, so rooms warm several times faster than physical air would.
- A lit fire needs air: each tick it checks the oxygen in its tile (below one mole counts as none; space and other airless tiles always do). After more than three seconds without oxygen it goes out with a popup, keeping its remaining fuel; the counter resets as soon as oxygen is back. Lighting it in vacuum works, it just dies a few seconds later.
- Putting it out (the fire size is the fuel stage, three equal parts of the cap: small, medium, large):
  - Feet: an alternative verb "stamp out". A small fire takes a 5 second do-after, a medium one 10 seconds (both break on movement and damage); a large one cannot be stamped out and sets the person alight instead (also if it grew to large during the do-after).
  - Water poured from a held container (bucket, glass, anything with a drainable solution, except extinguishers and spray bottles): 50 units of the extinguishing reagent (see below) put out a fire of any size and are taken from the container; with less there is a red popup and nothing is taken. Containers without water are ignored and the item is just placed. A splash of 50 or more units (e.g. a thrown bucket) does the same.
  - Spray (extinguisher, spray bottle, smaller splashes): the bonfire has an empty `Reactive` component so reagents touching it raise the reaction event, which the system reads. Particles of one spray arriving within about a second and a half count once. One spray puts out a small or medium fire; a large one needs two or three sprays (random, forgotten after ten seconds without a spray), the popup tells it is still burning.
  - Putting it out with a liquid (pouring, splash, spray, but not stamping) also plays a sizzle sound and puffs hot water vapor (373 K) into the air of its tile: 4, 8 or 16 moles for a small, medium or large fire, merged into the tile mixture. Tiles without real air (space, map atmosphere) get none.
  - The extinguishing reagents are a list on the component: the default is water only, and the bonfire prototype sets the 67 reagents that carry the extinguish effect (water, holy water, sodas, juices, coffee, tea and so on, plus one flammable one). Alcohol and other flammable reagents are not on it. Reagents added to the game later are not picked up automatically.
- Mobs are never heated; only items.

## Tuning notes
- Item heat power equals the electric grill and does not depend on fuel. Surroundings matter: items exchange heat with the air, so in vacuum or very cold air the fire's steady-state temperature can stay under the cooking thresholds; on the station it clears all of them.
- Not covered: burning mobs or items thrown in.

## Tests
- `CampfireTest` (integration, `_Maid`): feeding, lighting, cooking raw meat, burning out, an unlit fire not cooking, the examine level for each amount of fuel, the fuel rules, the air warming up to its ceiling in a sealed room, and going out without oxygen (and not in air). Tests that need a lit fire put air on the map, since the default test map is space.
